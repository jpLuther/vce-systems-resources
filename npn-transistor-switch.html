Try this

1. Raise Vin slowly from 0 V. At about what voltage does the LED start to glow, and why?

At about 0.7 V. The base-emitter junction behaves like a diode and needs roughly 0.7 V before any current flows into the base. Below that there is no base current, so no collector current.

2. Set Vin to 5 V with Rb = 10 kΩ. What limits the collector current?

The LED and collector resistor limit it, not the transistor: (5 V − 2 V − 0.2 V) ÷ 330 Ω is about 8.5 mA. The base current is large enough that the transistor could pass far more, so it is fully on (saturated).

3. Change Rb to 100 kΩ with Vin at 5 V. Why is the LED dimmer?

The base current drops to about 43 µA, so the collector current is only about 100 × 43 µA = 4.3 mA. That is less than the 8.5 mA the circuit allows, so the transistor stays in its active region and does not switch fully on. The base resistor is too large for a switch.

4. Why might a microcontroller use a transistor like this to drive a load?

A microcontroller pin can only supply a small current. A small base current from the pin can control a much larger collector current drawn from a separate supply, which is enough to run a bigger LED, a relay or a small motor.
